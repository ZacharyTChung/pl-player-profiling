## Title

Two Modes, Not Many Types: The Structure of Football Player Roles in Season-Aggregate Data

## Authors

Zachary Chung, Christian Chung

## Abstract

Studies of football player roles routinely report a set of discovered archetypes, but a partitioning algorithm returns a partition whether or not the data contain groups, so the usual evidence cannot distinguish a real taxonomy from a cut through a continuum. We test existence directly on 13,822 player seasons from 5 seasons of the big five European leagues, described by 33 statistics recovered from an archive of the data as it stood before its withdrawal from public access in January 2026. Cluster quality is calibrated against 25 simulated datasets matched on size, dimension and covariance but built to contain no groups. A two-mode division is real: the observed silhouette exceeds every simulation in every scope, by 18.0 standard deviations over all outfield players, and Hartigan's dip test rejects unimodality on the leading component at p < 0.0001. Finer structure is not, since beyond two modes the observed quality converges on the null and density based clustering labels every point noise within each position group. The modes are not positions relabelled, agreeing with position group at an adjusted Rand index of 0.293: 92.9 percent of defenders fall one side and 99.8 percent of forwards the other, while midfielders divide almost evenly. A classifier reaches 93.4 percent accuracy on the same features yet only 1.31 percent of its errors avoid midfield. The division transfers across leagues at a mean adjusted Rand index of 0.944. Football role space has two poles and a populated middle, and finer taxonomies are artefacts of the method used to find them.
