\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Every procedure below is seeded from a single constant, and every hyperparameter is
declared in one configuration file that the analysis code reads. Nothing is tuned after
seeing a result. All computation uses scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011scikit} unless
stated otherwise.

\subsection{Dimensionality reduction}

Principal component analysis is fitted to the standardised feature set, both globally and
separately within each position group. The global embedding is dominated by the axis that
separates defenders from forwards, which is the least interesting distinction available
because it is already recorded in the listed position, so the role profiling that follows
uses the position stratified fits.

Uniform manifold approximation and projection \citep{mcinnes2018umap} is fitted over a
grid of twelve configurations, with the number of neighbours in
\{10, 15, 30, 50\} crossed with a minimum distance in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.5\}. Configurations
are ranked by trustworthiness, which measures the degree to which points that are close
in the embedding were also close in the original space, and the full grid is reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:umap}. Selection by an explicit criterion matters here because
a manifold method will produce a visually appealing picture at almost any setting.

t-distributed stochastic neighbour embedding \citep{vandermaaten2008visualizing} is
computed at a perplexity of 30 for visual contrast only. It is never used as an input to
clustering, because its objective preserves local neighbourhoods at the expense of the
global geometry that a distance based clustering algorithm depends upon.

The three embeddings are compared on trustworthiness, on continuity, which is
trustworthiness computed with the roles of the two spaces exchanged, and on a nearest
neighbour position purity score at $k=10$ that reports the share of a player's ten
nearest neighbours that share his listed position group.

\subsection{Clustering}

Clusters are estimated with $k$-means as the primary method, with a Gaussian mixture
model whose Bayesian information criterion is reported across the same range of
component counts, and with HDBSCAN \citep{campello2013density}, for which we report the
fraction of players assigned to noise. Each is fitted both to the standardised features
and to the principal component space retaining ninety percent of variance, and each
reported result states which space produced it. Clustering is performed globally and
within each of the three outfield position groups.

The number of clusters is selected by a rule fixed in advance of any fitting, which
matters because four criteria applied after the fact can be made to justify almost any
choice. Over $k$ from 2 to 12 we compute the silhouette coefficient
\citep{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}, the Davies-Bouldin index, the Calinski-Harabasz index,
and the gap statistic \citep{tibshirani2001estimating}. The gap statistic has no
implementation in the library we use, so it is implemented here against the original
description, comparing the within-cluster dispersion to its expectation under fifty
reference datasets drawn uniformly over the bounding box of the data in the fitted space.
The rule selects the smallest $k$ whose silhouette lies within 0.02 of the best silhouette
observed and which ranks in the top three on at least two of the silhouette,
Calinski-Harabasz and gap criteria, with Davies-Bouldin breaking ties. Which criteria
agreed, and which did not, is reported alongside the choice.

\subsection{Stability and validation against listed positions}

A partition can be produced from any dataset, so the question that matters is whether it
survives resampling. We draw two hundred bootstrap resamples, refit the clustering on
each, and compute the adjusted Rand index \citep{hubert1985comparing} between each
bootstrap partition and the reference partition on the players common to both. We also
accumulate a consensus matrix recording, for every pair of players, the fraction of
resamples in which the two were assigned together. Clusters whose members co-assign below
0.6 on average are reported as unstable, and they are described as such in the results
rather than being renamed or merged to make the account tidier.

The relationship between the discovered clusters and the listed positions is quantified
by normalised mutual information and by the adjusted Rand index, computed against both
the four-way position group and the full position string. Players whose cluster
assignment disagrees with their listed group, and players whose Gaussian mixture
posterior is split across components, are extracted as candidate hybrids.

\subsection{Archetype profiling}

For every retained cluster we compute the centroid profile as a standardised deviation
from the mean of its position group, take the eight features with the largest absolute
deviation as the distinguishing features, and list the ten players closest to the
centroid and three players near the boundary. Archetype names are assigned only after
inspecting these profiles and the players they contain, and each name is accompanied by a
justification that cites the distinguishing features and the example players. The names
are hypotheses about what the statistical profile represents, not observations, and they
carry no authority beyond the profile that produced them.

Outliers are identified within position group with an isolation forest
\citep{liu2008isolation}, reported alongside a Mahalanobis distance so that a
distance-based and a partition-based notion of unusualness can be compared.

\subsection{Supervised validation}

Unsupervised structure is easy to over-interpret, so we ask a supervised model what
separates the listed positions. Logistic regression and gradient boosting are trained to
predict the position group from the feature set under five-fold stratified cross
validation, and we report accuracy, macro F1 and the confusion matrix. The purpose is
interpretability rather than accuracy: SHAP values \citep{lundberg2017unified} on the
boosted model identify which statistics actually distinguish each position in this
feature set. Players the classifier places in the wrong group are cross referenced
against the candidate hybrids from the clustering, since agreement between two methods
that fail in the same place is more informative than either alone.

A secondary ridge regression predicts non-penalty expected goals per ninety from the
non-shooting features only, so that the fitted value represents the shot volume a
player's role would ordinarily generate and the residual represents the part not
explained by role. We report the cross validated coefficient of determination and treat
the residual ranking with heavy caution, because a residual from a model with modest
explanatory power is mostly noise.

\subsection{Goalkeepers}

The goalkeeper analysis is severely constrained by the withdrawal described in
Section~\ref{sec:data:withdrawal}, which removed post-shot expected goals, sweeper
actions, distribution and cross handling. Only shot stopping and workload survive, so we
build two composites rather than the five that the surviving column headers appear to
promise. In place of post-shot expected goals we regress saves on shots on target faced
and use the residual as a shot stopping index. That index adjusts for the volume of work
a goalkeeper receives but not for its difficulty, which makes it a substantially weaker
instrument, and it is reported as such.

\subsection{Team signatures}

Each club is summarised in two ways: as a minutes-weighted centroid of its outfield
players in the shared principal component space, and as the share of its outfield minutes
played by each archetype. Clubs are then clustered hierarchically on archetype
composition and compared against final league position.

\subsection{Additional analyses}

Three further analyses address specific threats to the interpretation.

Player similarity is computed as cosine similarity within position group, and the nearest
neighbours of a set of well known players are reported so that a reader with football
knowledge can judge whether the geometry corresponds to anything real.

Cross-season replication refits the entire pipeline on the replication season and
computes the adjusted Rand index between the two seasons for players present in both,
which distinguishes archetypes that describe stable playing roles from artefacts of a
single season.

Per-90 rates computed over partial seasons are noisy, and that noise is largest exactly
where the minutes are fewest. We therefore apply empirical Bayes shrinkage toward the
position group mean, with the degree of shrinkage determined by minutes played
\citep{efron1977steins}, refit the clustering on the shrunken features, and report how
many assignments change. A partition that dissolves under shrinkage was substantially a
description of sampling noise.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

The analysis runs from a single command against pinned dependencies and a fixed seed.
Every figure and every number in this paper is generated by code in the accompanying
repository and read into the document from generated definitions at build time, so no
value in the text was transcribed by hand.
