\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Every procedure is seeded from a single constant, every hyperparameter is declared in one
configuration file the analysis code reads, and nothing is tuned after seeing a result.
Computation uses scikit-learn unless stated otherwise \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}. Procedural
detail a reader does not need in order to follow the argument is in
Section~\ref{S-sec:si:methods} of the supplement.

\subsection{Sample}
\label{sec:methods:data}

The analysis uses season-aggregate player statistics for the \ArcNLeagues{} major European
leagues over \ArcNSeasons{} seasons from \ArcSeasonFirst{} to \ArcSeasonLast{}, labelled by the
calendar year in which each season ends. It contains \ArcRows{} player-seasons, of which
\ArcEligible{} are outfield players above the minutes threshold of \MinMinutes{} and
\ArcKeepers{} are goalkeepers.

The tables originate from FBref, which published advanced statistics derived from a commercial
event-data provider \citep{fbref2026}. They are taken from a public mirror maintained by the
worldfootballR project rather than from the site directly, and that needs stating plainly
\citep{zivkovic2026worldfootballr}. On 20 January 2026 the site's operator removed every
statistic derived from that provider, retroactively and across completed seasons, after the
licence was terminated. The mirror predates the removal, so it is a secondary source in the
strict sense: a third party scraped it, we did not observe the scrape, and it cannot be
re-derived because the primary source no longer holds the data. What we can do, and do, is audit
it, with row counts and per-column and per-season coverage checked on load as part of the
pipeline. One season is excluded on those grounds, because the site restructured its
ball-carrying columns for the season ending 2023 and the mirror did not follow the renames,
leaving every carrying column empty while its neighbours are intact.

The outfield feature set contains \ArcFeatures{} per-90 features spanning six families: shot
volume and quality including non-penalty expected goals \citep{eggels2016explaining,
rathke2017examination}; chance creation; ball progression; touches across the five pitch zones;
take-ons; and defensive work with passing accuracy by distance.
Table~\ref{S-tab:archivefeatures} lists them with their source tables. Minutes and appearances
are kept as context and never enter the feature set, because squad usage describes a player's
opportunity rather than the role he performs within it.

Defensive actions can only occur while the opponent has the ball, so their raw rates confound
what a player does with how often his team is out of possession, which tracks team strength. The
conventional correction assumes defensive volume is proportional to time spent out of possession
\citep{knutson2014possession}. That is an empirical claim, and we measure it rather than assume
it: regressing log team defensive volume per ninety on log opponent possession over
\ArcPadjTeamSeasons{} team-seasons gives an elasticity per statistic, and each count is adjusted
by its own fitted value. Attacking statistics are deliberately left unadjusted, since they scale
with a team's own possession rather than the opponent's.
Section~\ref{S-sec:si:elasticity} reports the elasticities with bootstrap intervals and
per-league refits, and tests that asymmetry rather than asserting it.

A second sample is pulled live for the seasons ending 2025 and 2026, after the withdrawal, with
expected goals from Understat as an independent source \citep{robberechts2026soccerdata,
understat2026}. It retains \NFeatures{} features, roughly half the primary set, and serves only
as a robustness case, because a result holding on both a rich and an impoverished description of
the same game is worth more than one holding on either alone. It also carries a warning that
applies beyond this study: the affected tables are still served with complete column headers and
one row per player, and only the values are gone, so a feature set assembled from the published
schema resolves correctly and yields an empty matrix. Selecting features against observed values
rather than documentation is now a necessary step with this source.

Season aggregates discard context, and that bounds every claim below. A tackle count does not
say where on the pitch the tackles happened, and an expected goals total does not distinguish
six poor chances from two good ones. The sample is recent European club football, and the
clusters are descriptive: a label records how a player's season looked, not why.

\subsection{Clustering and the selection rule}
\label{sec:methods:selection}

Clusters are estimated with $k$-means, alongside a Gaussian mixture model whose Bayesian
information criterion is reported over the same range and HDBSCAN, which is permitted to answer
that nothing is clustered and does so by assigning points to noise \citep{campello2013density}.
Clustering runs globally and within each of the three outfield position groups, which are
treated separately because correlations between features reverse sign across groups, so one
pooled geometry would average over relationships pointing in opposite directions.

The number of clusters is chosen by a rule fixed before any fitting, which matters because four
criteria applied after the fact can be made to justify almost any choice. Over $k$ from 2 to 12
we compute the silhouette, the Davies-Bouldin index, the Calinski-Harabasz index and the gap
statistic \citep{rousseeuw1987silhouettes, tibshirani2001estimating}. The rule selects the
smallest $k$ whose silhouette lies within 0.02 of the best observed and which ranks in the top
three on at least two of the silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz and gap criteria, with Davies-Bouldin
breaking ties. Which criteria agreed is reported alongside the choice.

\subsection{Testing whether clusters exist at all}
\label{sec:methods:null}

Selecting a number of clusters presupposes that clusters are there to be counted, and that
presupposition is the weak point of the genre. The criteria above are computed on the partition
rather than against any alternative, so a silhouette of \StrObsAll{} sounds like evidence until
one asks what silhouette structureless data of the same shape would produce.

Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ be the standardised feature matrix for a scope, $\pi_k(X)$
the partition $k$-means returns under a fixed seed, and $s_k(X)$ its mean silhouette. The number
of clusters reported, $k^\ast = 2$, is not chosen by inspecting $s_k(X)$; it is returned by the
rule declared above, and the calibration then asks one question about it.

Three generators produce clusterless data matched to $X$. The Gaussian generator draws from
$\mathcal{N}(\hat\mu, \hat\Sigma)$, matching size, dimension and correlation while containing no
groups by construction. The copula generator reproduces every marginal distribution of $X$
exactly, skew and bounds included, along with the observed rank correlations, destroying only
the joint structure beyond them. The uniform generator fills the bounding box of $X$ after
rotation to its principal axes, which is the reference distribution of the gap statistic
\citep{tibshirani2001estimating}. A single Gaussian is the easiest of the three to beat, because
skew alone raises the silhouette a partition can reach, so whichever is hardest in a given scope
is treated as the calibration that counts. Section~\ref{S-sec:si:methods} states each generator
formally.

For each generator $g$ and each of $B = \StrSimulations{}$ draws $X^{(g,b)}$, the identical
pipeline computes $s_{k^\ast}(X^{(g,b)})$, giving a null sample of size $B$. The reported
statistic is the standard score
\[
  z_g \;=\; \frac{s_{k^\ast}(X) - \bar s_g}{\hat\sigma_g},
\]
taken against the mean and standard deviation of that null sample. With $B$ draws the null
standard deviation is itself an estimate, so $z_g$ describes the margin rather than serving as a
test statistic with a known distribution. The claim the paper makes is the distribution-free
one, that $s_{k^\ast}(X)$ exceeds every one of the $B$ null values under every generator, which
under exchangeability between the observed and simulated statistics has Monte Carlo probability
at most $1/(B+1)$ per generator and scope. The headline is a conjunction over three generators
and four scopes rather than a disjunction, so it can only be weakened by adding a generator or a
scope, never strengthened.

The same generators supply two further calibrations. Hartigan's dip statistic on the leading
principal component of each draw gives a null sample against which the observed dip is placed
the same way, which matters because at these sample sizes the dip test's p-value rejects
unimodality for departures too small to be of interest \citep{hartigan1985dip}. The bootstrap
adjusted Rand index of $\pi_{k^\ast}$ is computed on the simulated datasets by exactly the
resampling procedure applied to $X$ \citep{hubert1985comparing}, so the stability the genre
reports for real data can be read against the stability a clusterless continuum produces. Rather
than assert that a partition imposed on structureless data passes a bootstrap, the paper
measures it.

One assumption should be stated because the analysis makes it silently otherwise. The rows of
$X$ are player-seasons and the same player appears in up to \ArcNSeasons{} of them, so rows are
not independent. The generators match the marginal and pairwise structure of $X$ but not this
nesting, and the bootstrap resamples rows as if exchangeable. The replication below, which
refits every season and every league independently, does not share this assumption, and it is
why the paper rests no claim on a single pooled fit.

\subsection{Validation}

Five checks are applied to whatever the calibration accepts, each set out in full in
Section~\ref{S-sec:si:methods}. Stability comes from two hundred bootstrap resamples scored
against the reference partition. Replication is tested more stringently, since a resample reuses
the same players: each season and each league is standardised inside itself and clustered from
scratch, borrowing no centre, no scale and no starting point, and one scope's centroids are also
imported into another scope's players, which separates replication of the solution from change
in the players. Robustness to the description is tested by grouping the features into six
families, removing each in turn and trying each alone, and rerunning the selection rule and the
calibration every time. Agreement with the listed positions is quantified by the adjusted Rand
index and normalised mutual information, and independently by asking a gradient boosted
classifier to predict position group from the same features \citep{ke2017lightgbm,
lundberg2017unified}, where two properties of the confusion matrix are read as evidence about
geometry rather than accuracy: whether the two extreme classes are confused with each other, and
whether errors concentrate in the class between them. Finally the structure is tested against
what happened, by summarising each team-season by its minutes-weighted composition and
predicting points per match out of sample under leave-one-season-out, leave-one-league-out and
leave-one-club-out holdouts against possession as the baseline. The increment over possession is
the quantity of interest, because composition and dominance are confounded and only the
increment says whether the modes carry information that dominance does not.

Goalkeepers share almost no features with outfield players and are analysed separately on the
\KeeperFeatureCountArchive{} canonical goalkeeper features, under the same threshold,
standardisation and selection rule, with saves above expectation computed as post-shot expected
goals minus goals allowed so that shot difficulty is conditioned on rather than shot volume
alone \citep{vatvani2022upgrading}. One choice is declared in advance because the result turns
on it. The goalkeeper block mixes what a goalkeeper does with what his defence permits him to
face, and a partition fitted on both will separate teams rather than goalkeepers, so we fit
twice, once on the full block and once on technique alone, and report for each how much variance
membership explains in situational against technical quantities.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

The analysis runs from a single command against pinned dependencies and a fixed seed. Every
figure and every number in this paper and its supplement is generated by code in the
accompanying repository and read into the document from generated definitions at build time, so
no value was transcribed by hand. The code, the retrieval step that fetches the archive from its
stated upstream with a recorded digest per file, the cluster assignments for both samples and
the sources of both documents are at
\repourl{}.
