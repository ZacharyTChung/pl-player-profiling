\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Every procedure below is seeded from a single constant, every hyperparameter is declared in
one configuration file that the analysis code reads, and nothing is tuned after seeing a
result. Computation uses scikit-learn unless stated otherwise \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}.

\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:methods:data}

The analysis uses season-aggregate player statistics for the \ArcNLeagues{} major European
leagues, covering \ArcNSeasons{} seasons from \ArcSeasonFirst{} to \ArcSeasonLast{}, where a
season is labelled by the calendar year in which it ends. The sample contains \ArcRows{}
player-seasons, of which \ArcEligible{} are outfield players above the minutes threshold of
\MinMinutes{} and \ArcKeepers{} are goalkeepers.

The tables originate from FBref, which published advanced statistics derived from a commercial
event-data provider \citep{fbref2026}. They are obtained here from a public mirror maintained
by the worldfootballR project rather than from the site directly, and that choice needs stating
plainly \citep{zivkovic2026worldfootballr}. On 20 January 2026 the site's operator removed
every statistic derived from that provider, retroactively and across completed seasons, after
the licence was terminated. The mirror predates the removal. It is therefore a secondary source
in the strict sense: it was scraped by a third party, we did not observe the scrape, and it
cannot be re-derived from the primary source because the primary source no longer holds the
data. What we can do, and do, is audit it. Row counts, per-column coverage and per-season
coverage are checked on load, and the checks are part of the pipeline rather than a one-off
inspection. Retrieval is part of the pipeline as well, recording a SHA-256 digest per file
alongside row and column counts, so that an upstream change is detectable rather than silent.
One season is excluded on those grounds, because the site restructured its ball-carrying
columns for the season ending 2023 and the mirror did not follow the renames, leaving every
carrying column empty for that season while neighbouring columns are intact. The pipeline
raises rather than imputing whenever a feature exceeds half missing within any retained season,
so the exclusion is enforced by a check and not by a comment.

The outfield feature set contains \ArcFeatures{} features, listed in full with their source
table in Table~\ref{tab:archivefeatures}. It spans six families: shot volume and quality
including non-penalty expected goals \citep{eggels2016explaining, rathke2017examination};
chance creation; ball progression; touches across the five pitch zones; take-ons; and
defensive work together with passing accuracy by distance. Counting statistics are divided by
nineties played, and quantities published as rates are used as published. Minutes and
appearances are retained as context and never enter the feature set, because squad usage
describes a player's opportunity rather than the role he performs within it. Several features
are undefined for some players by construction rather than by data loss, since a player who
attempted no take-ons has no take-on success rate; after the minutes filter these affect at
most eight percent of players in any single feature, and are median-imputed within position
group and flagged.

A second sample is collected directly from the live site for the seasons ending 2025 and 2026,
after the withdrawal, with expected goals taken from Understat as an independent source
\citep{robberechts2026soccerdata, understat2026}. It retains \NFeatures{} features, roughly
half the primary set, and it is used only as a robustness case, because a result that holds on
both a rich and an impoverished description of the same game is worth more than one that holds
on either alone. That sample also carries a warning that applies beyond this study: the
affected tables are still served with complete column headers and one row per player, and only
the values are gone, so a feature set assembled from the published schema resolves correctly
and yields an empty matrix. Feature selection against observed values instead of documentation
is now a necessary step with this source. Section~\ref{sec:si:reduced} reports the audit and
the analyses that sample supports.

Season aggregates discard context, and that bounds every claim below. A tackle count does not
say where on the pitch the tackles happened, and an expected goals total does not distinguish
six poor chances from two good ones. The sample is recent European club football, so nothing
here should be read as a claim about the game in other eras or at other levels, and the
clusters are descriptive: a cluster label records how a player's season looked, not why.

\subsection{Adjusting defensive counts for possession}
\label{sec:methods:padj}

Defensive actions can only occur while the opponent has the ball, so a player in a side that
concedes possession accumulates interceptions, tackles and fouls partly through exposure rather
than through role, and since possession tracks team strength this confound would enter the
feature set as a proxy for the quality of a player's team. The correction introduced by
\citet{knutson2014possession}, in the proportional form in common use since, multiplies a
defensive rate by the ratio of a reference opponent possession to the actual one, which assumes
defensive volume is directly proportional to time spent out of possession. That is an empirical
claim rather than a definition. Regressing the logarithm of team defensive volume per ninety on
the logarithm of opponent possession over \ArcPadjTeamSeasons{} team-seasons gives the
elasticity, the percentage change in defensive volume produced by a one percent change in
opponent possession, for which direct proportionality corresponds to a value of one.

We adjust with the measured elasticity instead of the assumed one, raising the exposure ratio
to the power of each statistic's own fitted value. The exponent is estimated from team totals
rather than player rates, because the quantity being corrected is a team-level exposure effect,
and it is pooled across seasons and leagues, because it is a nuisance parameter that uses no
player-level information and so cannot manufacture the cross-season and cross-league agreement
reported later. Elasticities are reported with bootstrap intervals from \ElastNBootstrap{}
resamples of the team-seasons, refitted within every league and every season separately, and
checked against a model with a quadratic term in log possession. Attacking statistics are
deliberately left unadjusted, since they scale with a team's own possession rather than the
opponent's; Section~\ref{sec:si:elasticity} reports the elasticities in full and tests that
asymmetry rather than asserting it.

\subsection{Clustering and the selection rule}
\label{sec:methods:selection}

Clusters are estimated with $k$-means as the primary method, with a Gaussian mixture model
whose Bayesian information criterion is reported across the same range of component counts, and
with HDBSCAN, for which we report the fraction of players assigned to noise
\citep{campello2013density}. Clustering is performed globally and within each of the three
outfield position groups, which are treated separately because correlations between features
reverse sign across groups, so a single pooled geometry averages over relationships that point
in opposite directions.

The number of clusters is selected by a rule fixed in advance of any fitting, which matters
because four criteria applied after the fact can be made to justify almost any choice. Over $k$
from 2 to 12 we compute the silhouette coefficient, the Davies-Bouldin index, the
Calinski-Harabasz index and the gap statistic \citep{rousseeuw1987silhouettes,
tibshirani2001estimating}. The rule selects the smallest $k$ whose silhouette lies within 0.02
of the best silhouette observed and which ranks in the top three on at least two of the
silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz and gap criteria, with Davies-Bouldin breaking ties. Which
criteria agreed, and which did not, is reported alongside the choice.

\subsection{Testing whether clusters exist at all}
\label{sec:methods:null}

Selecting a number of clusters presupposes that clusters are there to be counted, and that
presupposition is the weak point of the genre. The internal criteria used to choose $k$ are
computed on the partition rather than against any alternative, so a silhouette of \StrObsAll{}
sounds like evidence until one asks what silhouette structureless data of the same shape would
have produced.

Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ be the standardised feature matrix for a scope, with $n$
player-seasons and $d = \ArcFeatures{}$ features, and let $\pi_k(X)$ be the partition returned
by $k$-means with $k$ clusters under a fixed seed and multiple restarts. Let $s_k(X)$ be the
mean silhouette of $\pi_k(X)$, computed on a fixed subsample of at most four thousand rows when
$n$ exceeds that, since the silhouette is quadratic in $n$. The number of clusters reported,
$k^\ast = 2$, is not chosen by inspecting $s_k(X)$. It is returned by the selection rule
declared above, and the calibration then asks a single question about it.

Three generators produce clusterless data matched to $X$. The Gaussian generator draws $n$ rows
from $\mathcal{N}(\hat\mu, \hat\Sigma)$ with the empirical mean and covariance, matching the
data in size, dimension and correlation while containing no group structure by construction.
The copula generator transforms each column of $X$ to normal scores by rank, estimates the
correlation $\hat R$ of those scores, draws $n$ rows from $\mathcal{N}(0, \hat R)$, and maps
each simulated column back through the empirical quantile function of the corresponding
observed column, so that every marginal distribution of $X$ is reproduced exactly, skew and
bounds included, and only the joint structure beyond rank correlation is destroyed. The uniform
generator rotates $X$ to its principal axes, draws $n$ rows uniformly within the resulting
bounding box, and rotates back, which is the reference distribution of
\citet{tibshirani2001estimating}. A single Gaussian is the easiest of the three to beat,
because skew alone raises the silhouette a partition can reach, so the paper treats whichever
is hardest in a given scope as the calibration that counts.

For each generator $g$ and each of $B = \StrSimulations{}$ draws $X^{(g,b)}$, the identical
pipeline computes $s_{k^\ast}(X^{(g,b)})$, giving a null sample of size $B$ per generator. The
reported statistic is the standard score
\[
  z_g \;=\; \frac{s_{k^\ast}(X) - \bar s_g}{\hat\sigma_g},
\]
where $\bar s_g$ and $\hat\sigma_g$ are the mean and standard deviation of that null sample.
With $B$ draws the null standard deviation is itself an estimate, so $z_g$ describes the margin
rather than serving as a test statistic with a known distribution. The claim the paper makes is
the distribution-free one, that $s_{k^\ast}(X)$ exceeds every one of the $B$ null values under
every generator, which under the null of exchangeability between the observed and simulated
statistics has Monte Carlo probability at most $1/(B+1)$ per generator and scope. The headline
is a conjunction over three generators and four scopes rather than a disjunction, so it is
conservative with respect to multiplicity: it can only be weakened by adding a generator or a
scope, never strengthened.

The same generators supply two further calibrations. Hartigan's dip statistic on the leading
principal component of each $X^{(g,b)}$ gives a null sample against which the observed dip is
placed in the same way, which matters because at these sample sizes the dip test's p-value
rejects unimodality for departures too small to be of interest \citep{hartigan1985dip}. The
bootstrap adjusted Rand index of $\pi_{k^\ast}$ is computed on the simulated datasets by exactly
the resampling procedure applied to $X$ \citep{hubert1985comparing}, so that the stability the
genre reports for real data can be read against the stability a clusterless continuum produces.
Rather than assert that a partition imposed on structureless data passes a bootstrap, the paper
measures it.

One assumption should be stated because the analysis makes it silently otherwise. The rows of
$X$ are player-seasons, and the same player appears in up to \ArcNSeasons{} of them, so rows
are not independent. The generators match the marginal and pairwise structure of $X$ but not
this nesting, and the bootstrap resamples rows as if exchangeable. The replication analysis
below, which refits every season and every league independently, does not share this
assumption, and it is the reason the paper does not rest the two-mode claim on any single
pooled fit.

\subsection{Stability, replication and the listed positions}

We draw two hundred bootstrap resamples, refit the clustering on each, and compute the adjusted
Rand index between each bootstrap partition and the reference partition on the players common
to both, accumulating a consensus matrix of pairwise co-assignment alongside it. Replication is
tested separately and more stringently, because a resample reuses the same players. Each season
and each league is standardised inside itself and clustered from scratch, borrowing no centre,
no scale and no starting point from the pooled fit. Cluster labels are arbitrary per fit, so
labels are aligned to the pooled partition on the contingency table before any label-dependent
quantity is computed, and an assertion fails the run if an aligned agreement drops to chance.
Three comparisons are reported because they answer different questions: an independent fit
scored against the pooled partition on the same rows; one season's labels scored against
another's on the players present in both, which mixes replication with real change in those
players; and one scope's centroids imported into another scope's players and scored against
that scope's own fit, which isolates replication. Each scope's centroid-difference vector is
also correlated with the pooled one, to check that the scopes recover the same axis rather than
a similarly sized partition. Agreement with the listed positions is quantified by normalised
mutual information and by the adjusted Rand index, against both the position group and the full
position string.

A two-mode result on \ArcFeatures{} features could in principle be carried by one block of
them, so the features are grouped into six families, with the grouping stated in the analysis
code and asserted to cover every feature exactly once. Each family is removed in turn and each
is tried alone, with the selection rule and the null calibration rerun on what remains in every
scope. For every ablation we record the number of clusters selected, the standard score against
the null, and the adjusted Rand index between the ablated partition and the full one, so that a
surviving $k$ of two can be distinguished from the same two modes surviving.

\subsection{Validation against results}

The structure is tested against what happened. The archive's team tables carry goals but not
results, so wins, draws and losses are recovered by summing the goalkeeper table, which credits
every match to the goalkeeper who played it, and the recovery is audited against the schedule
with the exceptions listed. Each team-season is summarised by its minutes-weighted composition
on the two-mode partition and on the six archetypes, and by the minutes-weighted mean position
of its players along the axis between the two centroids. Prediction is out of sample under
three holdouts, one season at a time, one league at a time and one club at a time, the last
being the strict one since no held-out team-season then shares a club with the training data,
by ridge regression with the penalty chosen by cross-validation inside the training folds,
comparing possession alone, the composition alone and both. The increment of the last over the
first is the quantity of interest, because composition and dominance are confounded and only
the increment says whether the modes carry information about results that dominance does not.

\subsection{Profiling, supervised validation and goalkeepers}

For every retained cluster we compute the centroid profile as a standardised deviation from the
mean of its position group, take the eight features with the largest absolute deviation as the
distinguishing features, and list the players closest to the centroid and near the boundary.
Archetype names are assigned only after inspecting these profiles and the players they contain,
and each name carries a justification citing the distinguishing features and the example
players. The names are hypotheses about what a statistical profile represents, not
observations. Outliers are identified within position group with an isolation forest alongside
a Mahalanobis distance, so that a partition-based and a distance-based notion of unusualness
can be compared \citep{liu2008isolation}.

Unsupervised structure is easy to over-interpret, so we also ask a supervised model what
separates the listed positions. Logistic regression and gradient boosting are trained to
predict the position group from the same features under five-fold stratified cross-validation
against a majority-class baseline running through identical folds \citep{ke2017lightgbm}, and
SHAP values on the boosted model identify which statistics do the separating
\citep{lundberg2017unified}. Two properties of the confusion matrix are read as evidence about
geometry rather than about accuracy: whether the two extreme classes are confused with each
other, and whether the errors concentrate in the class that lies between them. An ordered space
with two poles produces few errors of the first kind and many of the second, while three
genuine categories would not.

Goalkeepers share almost no features with outfield players and are analysed with a separate
pipeline on the \KeeperFeatureCountArchive{} canonical goalkeeper features, under the same
threshold, standardisation and selection rule. Saves above expectation are computed as
post-shot expected goals minus goals allowed, which conditions on the difficulty of the shots
faced rather than only on their number \citep{vatvani2022upgrading}. One choice needs stating
in advance because the result turns on it. The goalkeeper feature block mixes quantities
describing what a goalkeeper does with quantities describing what his defence permits him to
face, and a partition fitted on both will separate teams rather than goalkeepers. We therefore
fit twice, once on the full block and once on technique alone with the exposure variables
excluded, and report for each partition how much variance membership explains in situational
quantities against technical ones.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

The analysis runs from a single command against pinned dependencies and a fixed seed. Every
figure and every number in this paper is generated by code in the accompanying repository and
read into the document from generated definitions at build time, so no value in the text was
transcribed by hand. The code, the retrieval step that fetches the archive from its stated
upstream with a recorded digest per file, the cluster assignments for both samples, and the
sources of this document are at
\url{https://github.com/ZacharyTChung/pl-player-profiling}.
