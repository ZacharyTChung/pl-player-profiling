\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Is there any structure at all?}
\label{sec:results:structure}

The first question is not how many clusters there are but whether there are any.
Figure~\ref{fig:structurenull} answers it against the three clusterless references.

Real structure exists, and it is confined to a single division. At two clusters the observed
silhouette is \StrObsAll{} for the outfield pool against a Gaussian null mean of \StrNullAll{},
and it exceeds every one of the \StrSimulations{} simulations; the same holds in each position
group. A Gaussian is the softest reference available, so the copula null, which keeps every
feature's real marginal distribution and rank correlations and so removes the objection that skew
alone produced the separation, is the one to read. It is the hardest of the three in every scope,
and the observed partition clears it everywhere, by \StrZCopulaAll{} standard deviations on the
pool and by \StrZCopulaDF{}, \StrZCopulaMF{} and \StrZCopulaFW{} within the three position
groups. The observed silhouette at two clusters exceeds \StrBeatsEverywhere{} simulation under
\StrBeatsEverywhere{} null in \StrBeatsEverywhere{} scope, and the smallest margin anywhere, over
all four scopes and all three nulls, is \StrMinZOverall{} standard deviations.

Beyond two clusters the curves converge, which is the central negative result stated precisely.
By around six clusters the observed silhouette lies inside the simulated band in every position
group, so the third and subsequent clusters a taxonomy would name are not distinguishable from
what a partitioning algorithm extracts from structureless data.

The second standard piece of evidence for a published taxonomy is a bootstrap, and the same
simulations show what it is worth. Computed on clusterless datasets by exactly the procedure
applied to the real data, the bootstrap adjusted Rand index of the two-cluster partition never
falls below \StrBootNullMinSingle{} on any clusterless dataset in any scope, against
\StrBootObsMin{} to \StrBootObsMax{} on the real data. The gap is as small as \StrBootGapMin{},
and a stability screen at \StrBootScreen{}, demanding by the genre's standards, is passed by
\StrBootNullPassesScreen{} clusterless dataset in \StrBootNullPassesScreen{} scope. A resampling
check does not distinguish real structure from a partitioned continuum: it measures whether an
algorithm is deterministic on similar inputs, which $k$-means is.

Three further instruments agree, with one exception reported as such. The observed dip statistic
on the leading principal component sits \StrDipMinZAll{} null standard deviations above the
hardest reference for the pool, \StrDipMinZDF{} for defenders and \StrDipMinZMF{} for
midfielders, so the dominant axis is two-moded rather than merely wide. Forwards are the
exception: the dip test returns $p=\StrDipPFW{}$ and the observed dip sits \StrDipMinZFW{}
standard deviations against the uniform reference, which is to say below it. The forward split is
real by the silhouette calibration, clearing the hardest null by \StrMinZFW{}, and its leading
axis is not bimodal by the dip. Both halves are reported because together they say what the
forward split is, a cut through a continuum of involvement that the null cannot produce rather
than two kinds of forward. Density-based clustering assigns \StrNoisePctAll{} percent of the pool to noise
and every position group entirely to noise, which is what a density method does when handed a
continuum with a density gradient instead of separated groups, and the mixture criterion declines
monotonically to the top of the candidate range in every scope, the signature of a model
absorbing extra components into a smooth density.

Football role data contains a real division into two modes. It does not contain a taxonomy.

\subsection{The shape of the space, and what the modes are not}
\label{sec:results:geometry}

A two-mode description invites two objections. If the data reduce to two groups, perhaps they
reduce to one number; and if not, perhaps the two groups are the listed positions under another
name. Figure~\ref{fig:geometry} answers both, and the answer to each is no.

The leading principal component carries \ArcPCOne{} percent of the variance, far more than any
other single direction, which confirms that one axis dominates. It is nowhere near the whole
space: the first three components together reach only \ArcPCThree{} percent and \ArcPCNinety{}
are needed to reach 90 percent of the variance, so the role space has one dominant axis and a long
tail of smaller ones, which is a different object from a line. The content matches the football.
The first component separates players by where on the pitch they touch the ball, loading
positively on progressive receptions and touches in the attacking third and the opposition box
and negatively on touches in the defensive third and in the defending team's own box. The second
is a progression axis close to orthogonal to it, so a deep midfielder who moves the ball forward
and a striker who receives it in the box sit at opposite ends of the second while sharing
territory on the first. The first component is bimodal and drives the partition; the components
behind it carry variation that is real, interpretable and continuous.

Projecting every player onto the axis between the two centroids shows the distribution the paper
is describing: density concentrated at two ends with a populated middle, and the listed positions
laid across that shape rather than aligned with it. Agreement with the listed position group is
well above chance and well below recovery, at an adjusted Rand index of \ArcARIvsPosition{} and a
normalised mutual information of \ArcNMIvsPosition{}, where a relabelling of position would return
values near one and an unrelated partition values near zero. Where the line falls is the
informative part. \ArcSplitDF{} percent of defenders fall on one side and \ArcSplitFW{} percent of
forwards on the other, while midfielders divide almost evenly, \ArcSplitMF{} percent of
them on the attacking side, and it is that even split which drags the agreement indices down. The
partition is not a relabelling of position but a cut through it: the two extremes are assigned
almost deterministically and the group between them is assigned by which end it leans toward.

A supervised model recovers the same geometry from machinery that shares nothing with the
clustering. Gradient boosting predicts the listed position group at \SupGBMAcc{} percent accuracy
out of fold against a majority-class baseline of \SupBaseline{} percent, with multinomial logistic
regression barely behind at \SupLogitAcc{} percent; the near equality of a linear model and a
boosted ensemble is itself informative, since a taxonomy of many discrete types would leave more
interaction structure for the trees to find. The errors are the argument. Over \SupN{}
player-seasons, only \SupPoleErrors{} confusions link the two extreme classes directly, against
\SupMiddleErrors{} that run through midfield, so \SupPoleShare{} percent of misclassifications
skip the middle. That is the confusion structure of
an ordered line whose ends are far apart and whose middle overlaps both, not of three distinct
categories. The attributions agree: the three strongest features overall are \ShapArcTopOne{},
\ShapArcTopTwo{} and \ShapArcTopThree{}, all territorial rather than technical. Position, as the
data record it, is mostly a statement about where a player stands.

\subsection{What the two modes are}
\label{sec:results:archetypes}

Two modes within each of three position groups give six descriptions. Names were assigned only
after inspecting centroid profiles and member players, and the justifications are generated from
the computed centroids rather than composed for the paper. Figure~\ref{fig:modes} shows the
features that separate each pair.

Among defenders the division is between \ArcNDFZero{} \ArcNameDFZero{} and \ArcNDFOne{}
\ArcNameDFOne{}. The first take three times as many touches in their own defensive third, more
than twice as many in their own box, twice the possession-adjusted clearances and win
considerably more aerial duels; the second take five times as many touches in the attacking
third, attempt ten times as many crosses and four times as many take-ons, and receive far more
progressive passes. This is the centre back against wide defender distinction, and the notable
point is that the source's own position label does not encode it, since the great majority of both
groups are listed simply as defenders.

Among midfielders the division separates \ArcNMFZero{} \ArcNameMFZero{} from \ArcNMFOne{}
\ArcNameMFOne{}. The first recover the ball more often, tackle more and take far more touches in
their own third. The instructive detail is that they also make more passes into the final third
and more progressive passes than the second group: they are not less involved in progressing the
ball, they progress it differently, by passing forward from deep rather than by receiving high. A
description treating attacking involvement as one axis would miss that, and it is the clearest
case in the paper of the richer feature set buying something a poorer one could not.

Among forwards the division separates \ArcNFWZero{} \ArcNameFWZero{} from \ArcNFWOne{}
\ArcNameFWOne{}, and it is not a division by scoring, since non-penalty expected goals are
slightly higher in the first group. What separates them is carrying and creation, with the second
recording nearly three times the progressive carries, almost three times the passes into the box
and twice the take-ons while shooting slightly less. Forward is not one role, but the axis that
splits it is involvement in build-up rather than goal threat.

One structural property constrains how far the six read as independent. Features are standardised
within position group before a two-way split, so the two centroids in a group are exactly
anti-parallel and the larger cluster necessarily carries the smaller deviations. Each pair is the
two ends of one axis, and the six archetypes describe three axes rather than six independently
shaped profiles. The three are also not equally supported. The defender split is strongest on
every measure applied to it, the midfield split is well supported with a more populated boundary,
and the forward split is the weakest and should be read with the most caution, since its dip test
does not reject unimodality, the division is uneven, and one league's refit falls well below the
others.

\subsection{The same division in every season, league and feature family}
\label{sec:results:replication}

A partition that appears once describes one dataset; a partition that appears independently in
five seasons and five competitions describes the sport. Each season and each league is therefore
standardised inside itself and clustered from scratch, borrowing nothing from the pooled fit.

Every scope recovers the pooled partition. The weakest season agrees with the pool at an adjusted
Rand index of \RepSeasonPooledMin{} and the weakest league at \RepLeaguePooledMin{}, on \RepN{}
player-seasons, and the correlation between each scope's own discriminant direction and the pooled
direction never falls below \RepAxisCorrMin{}, so the separating axis is the same object each time
rather than a similarly sized one. Importing one scope's centroids into another scope's players is
the cleaner test, since it separates replication of the solution from change in the players:
across the twenty ordered league pairs the transferred centroids reproduce the receiving league's
own fit at a mean adjusted Rand index of \RepTransferMean{}, never below \RepTransferMin{}. A
model of role fitted on Ligue 1 assigns Bundesliga players almost exactly as one fitted on
Bundesliga does. Season against season on players present in both is lower, at \RepPairMean{}, and
the pattern explains why: agreement decays monotonically with the gap between seasons while the
transfer index over the same pairs stays flat, so the decay is players changing role over four
years. Across those pairs \RepShareMean{} percent of players are assigned to the same mode in both
seasons, so roughly one player in twelve moves between modes over a four-year span, a plausible
rate of genuine role change rather than the churn an unstable partition would produce.

A result on \ArcFeatures{} features could also be carried by one block of them, and a reviewer who
suspected the modes were the expected goals block wearing a costume would be right to ask.
Removing any one of the \AblFamiliesWord{} families removes nothing that matters: the rule returns two
clusters in \AblSurvivesAll{} leave-one-out in every scope, \AblCellsBeatNull{} of the
\AblCells{} feature sets tested exceed every one of the \AblSimulations{} clusterless simulations,
and the adjusted Rand index between each ablated partition and the full one runs from
\AblLeaveARIMin{} to \AblLeaveARIMax{}, so the survivors are the same two modes rather than two
different ones. The converse is more instructive than the headline. Most families produce a
two-cluster solution that beats the null when used alone, but agreement with the full partition
shows what those solutions are: shooting alone in defenders separates from the null at
\AblKeepZShootingDF{} standard deviations while agreeing with the paper's partition at only
\AblKeepARIShootingDF{}, so it finds the finishers against everyone else, not the deep against the
wide. Only territory reproduces the paper's own modes by itself in every scope, at an adjusted
Rand index from \AblTerritoryARIMin{} to \AblTerritoryARIMax{}. The two modes are where a player
touches the ball, and every other family either sharpens that division or, alone, cuts a different
one. Figure~\ref{fig:replication} shows the refits above and the ablation below, and
Table~\ref{S-tab:ablation} gives every cell.

\subsection{Do the modes predict results?}
\label{sec:results:outcomes}

Everything above is a claim about the structure of the data. The archive also carries what
happened, so the modes can be tested against real results. Each of \OutN{} team-seasons is
summarised by the minutes-weighted share of its outfield minutes in the attacking mode and by the
mean position of its players along the axis, with eligible players covering a median
\OutCoverageMedian{} percent of each club's outfield minutes.

The share correlates with points per match at a Spearman rank correlation of \OutRhoShare{}
(\OutRhoShareLow{} to \OutRhoShareHigh{}), and the continuous axis position is stronger at
\OutRhoAxis{}, which is what a continuum should show: where a club's players sit along the axis
carries more than which side of the cut they fall on. Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} lists the ten
team-seasons at the extremes so a reader can check them against memory, and they are the sides one
would name.

A correlation between attacking composition and points is partly circular, because good teams have
good attackers, so the test that matters is whether the modes carry anything beyond team
possession. Ridge regression of points per match on possession alone, the composition alone and
both gives out-of-sample coefficients of determination of \OutRSqPossSeason{}, \OutRSqCompSeason{}
and \OutRSqBothSeason{} holding out one season at a time, and \OutRSqPossClub{},
\OutRSqCompClub{} and \OutRSqBothClub{} under the strictest holdout, one club at a time, where no
held-out team-season shares a club with the training data. The modes add \OutIncSeason{}
(\OutIncSeasonLow{} to \OutIncSeasonHigh{}) to what possession explains under the first and
\OutIncClub{} under the last. The composition alone predicts slightly worse than possession alone,
which should be said plainly: the modes are not a substitute for dominance, they are information
about how it is produced that dominance does not contain.

Where the information lives is the sharpest result here. Possession plus the two two-mode measures
alone reach \OutRSqPossTwoModeSeason{}, an increment of \OutIncTwoModeSeason{} over possession
that is the whole of the gain. Adding the six archetype shares on top adds \OutIncArcheSeason{}
(\OutIncArcheSeasonLow{} to \OutIncArcheSeasonHigh{}), an interval that contains zero. Real
results say what the null calibration said: two modes carry information and a finer taxonomy
carries none the two modes did not already carry. Figure~\ref{fig:outcomes} shows the share
against points and the out of sample comparison.

\subsection{Goalkeepers}
\label{sec:results:keepers}

Goalkeepers are described by a different vocabulary, and the primary sample supplies all of it
across \ArcKeeperSeasons{} keeper-seasons. The first attempt at a partition walks into a trap, and
the trap is itself a finding. Clustering on all available features returns \ArcKeeperK{} clusters
that look interpretable until one asks what they are made of: membership explains a mean of
\ArcKeeperEtaFullSituation{} of the variance in situational quantities, the volume and quality of
shooting a defence permits, against \ArcKeeperEtaFullTechnique{} in quantities describing what the
goalkeeper does. The partition is largely a ranking of the teams in front of the goalkeepers.
Named as archetypes it would be a league table wearing a tactical vocabulary. Clustering on
technique alone reverses that completely: the same rule again returns \ArcKeeperK{} clusters, at a
bootstrap adjusted Rand index of \ArcKeeperBootARI{}, and membership now explains
\ArcKeeperEtaTechTechnique{} of the variance in technique against \ArcKeeperEtaTechSituation{} in
situation, a ratio of about seven.

What that partition describes is not the expected taxonomy. The familiar trio of shot stopper,
sweeper keeper and distributor is not what the data produces. The division is between
\ArcKeeperNameZero{}s, \ArcKeeperNZero{} keeper-seasons who stay on their line, and
\ArcKeeperNameOne{}s, \ArcKeeperNOne{} who come off it, separating on cross stopping and sweeping
together, while distribution style and shot stopping ability barely separate the clusters at all
and lie as continua across the division rather than as dimensions of it. The results test
discriminates between the two partitions in the direction the effect sizes predict: the
full-feature partition's share of a club's goalkeeper minutes correlates with points per match at
\OutKeepFull{}, which is what a partition sorting keepers by the team in front of them must do,
while the technique partition's correlates at \OutKeepTech{} (\OutKeepTechLow{} to
\OutKeepTechHigh{}), an interval barely clearing zero. The line keeper against sweeper claimer
division is unrelated to how good the team is, which is the property a description of goalkeeping
rather than of goalkeepers' teams should have, and it echoes the outfield result: one binary
distinction plus continuous variation, not a set of types.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:results:robustness}

Four checks bound what the result depends on, each reported in full in the supplement.

It does not depend on the richness of the description. The reduced sample is an unusually clean
test, because the withdrawal removed a specific and large part of the feature set without touching
the population, and repeating the analysis there returns two clusters globally and within every
position group, agreement with listed positions still weak at \ARIvsPosition{}, and density-based
clustering again assigning every position group entirely to noise. That also bounds what richer
data buys: it sharpens the description considerably but reveals no additional modes the poorer one
was hiding. It does not depend on the eligibility threshold either, since repeating the whole
selection and stability procedure at 270, \MinMinutes{} and 900 minutes returns two clusters at
every threshold with the silhouette moving only from \SensSilhouetteLow{} to
\SensSilhouetteHigh{} (Section~\ref{S-sec:si:sensitivity}).

The possession adjustment behind the defensive features is measured rather than assumed, and the
measurement matters: \NElastExcludeOne{} of the five defensive elasticities have bootstrap
intervals excluding one, so the conventional proportional correction overcorrects them, and applying it reverses the
sign of the residual correlation with team possession rather than removing it. The correction is
also one sided, and that asymmetry is tested rather than asserted
(Section~\ref{S-sec:si:elasticity}). Finally the partitions are not artefacts of sampling noise
in per-90 rates computed over partial seasons, since empirical Bayes shrinkage toward the position
group mean leaves the global partition essentially unchanged while the within-group splits survive
unequally; Section~\ref{S-sec:si:reduced} reports that and the within-season role drift analysis
that bounds the assumption that a player's role is constant across a season.
