In January 2026 the largest free source of advanced football statistics removed every
metric derived from its commercial provider, retroactively and without replacement.
Expected goals, passing volume, carrying, chance creation and the entire advanced
goalkeeping table ceased to be publicly available. We measure what player role structure
survives that withdrawal. Merging the statistics that remain with an independent expected
goals source yields \NFeatures{} features for \NEligiblePrimary{} Premier League outfield
players, on which we run dimensionality reduction, clustering under a selection rule fixed
in advance, bootstrap stability, supervised validation, and a replication on a second
season. Three results hold. Correlations reverse sign across position groups for
\NPairsSignFlip{} of \NPairsTotal{} feature pairs, so pooled correlation analysis describes
no real population. The data supports one binary division, attacking against defensive
involvement, which cuts across listed positions at an adjusted Rand index of only
\ARIvsPosition{} while a supervised model reaches \LGBMAccuracy{} accuracy on the same
features. That global division survives shrinkage toward the position mean at
\ShrinkARIGlobal{}, whereas the divisions within defender and forward groups fall to
\ShrinkARIDF{} and \ShrinkARIFW{} and are substantially descriptions of sampling noise.
Public football data now supports coarser role analysis than it did a year ago.
