Studies of football player roles routinely report a set of discovered archetypes, but a
partitioning algorithm returns a partition whether or not the data contain groups, so the
usual evidence cannot distinguish a real taxonomy from a cut through a continuum. We test
existence directly on \ArcRows{} player seasons from \ArcNSeasons{} seasons of the big five
European leagues, described by \ArcFeatures{} statistics recovered from an archive of the
data as it stood before its withdrawal from public access in January 2026. Cluster quality is
calibrated against \StrSimulations{} simulated datasets matched on size, dimension and
covariance but built to contain no groups. A two-mode division is real: the observed
silhouette exceeds every simulation in every scope, by \StrZAll{} standard deviations over
all outfield players, and Hartigan's dip test rejects unimodality on the leading component at
\StrDipPAll{}. Finer structure is not, since beyond two modes the observed quality converges
on the null and density based clustering labels every point noise within each position group.
The modes are not positions relabelled, agreeing with position group at an adjusted Rand
index of \ArcARIvsPosition{}: \ArcSplitDF{} percent of defenders fall one side and
\ArcSplitFW{} percent of forwards the other, while midfielders divide almost evenly. A
classifier reaches \SupGBMAcc{} percent accuracy on the same features yet only
\SupPoleShare{} percent of its errors avoid midfield. The division transfers across leagues
at a mean adjusted Rand index of \RepTransferMean{}. Football role space has two poles and a
populated middle, and finer taxonomies are artefacts of the method used to find them.
