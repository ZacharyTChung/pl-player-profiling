\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Primary sample}

The analysis uses season-aggregate player statistics for the \ArcNLeagues{} major European
leagues, covering \ArcNSeasons{} seasons from \ArcSeasonFirst{} to \ArcSeasonLast{}, where
a season is labelled by the calendar year in which it ends. The sample contains
\ArcRows{} player-seasons, of which \ArcEligible{} are outfield players meeting the minutes
threshold and \ArcKeepers{} are goalkeepers.

These tables originate from FBref, which published advanced statistics derived from a
commercial event-data provider. They are obtained here from a public mirror maintained by
the worldfootballR project \citep{zivkovic2026worldfootballr} rather than from the site
directly, and that choice needs stating plainly rather than burying. On 20 January 2026 the site's operator removed every
statistic derived from that provider, retroactively and across completed seasons, after the
licence was terminated. The mirror predates the removal. It is therefore a secondary source
in the strict sense, and a reader should weigh it accordingly: it was scraped by a third
party, we did not observe the scrape, and we cannot re-derive it from the primary source
because the primary source no longer holds the data. What we can do, and do, is audit it.
Row counts, per-column coverage and per-season coverage are checked on load, and the checks
are part of the pipeline rather than a one-off inspection.

One season is excluded on those grounds. FBref restructured its ball-carrying columns for
the season ending 2023 and the mirror did not follow the renames, so every carrying column
is empty for that season while neighbouring columns are intact. An overall missingness rate
hides this, because the affected columns are complete in the five retained seasons; a
per-season rate exposes it immediately. The pipeline raises rather than imputing whenever a
feature exceeds half missing within any retained season, so the exclusion is enforced by a
check rather than by a comment.

Retrieval is part of the pipeline. The module \texttt{src/archive\_fetch.py} downloads each
upstream file from the stated repository, converts it, and records a SHA-256 digest per file
in a manifest alongside row and column counts, so that a run can be identified after the fact
and an upstream change is detectable rather than silent. The mirror rewrites its files in
place on each scrape and publishes no tags, so the digests are the only available pin.

\subsection{Feature set}

The outfield feature set contains \ArcFeatures{} features and is listed in full in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:features}. It spans six families: shot volume and quality
including non-penalty expected goals; chance creation including expected assisted goals,
key passes and shot and goal creating actions; ball progression through progressive passes,
carries and receptions and entries into the final third and penalty area; touches broken
down across the five pitch zones; take-ons attempted and their success rate; and defensive
work through tackles, interceptions, blocks, clearances, ball recoveries and aerial duel
success, together with passing accuracy at short, medium and long range.

Counting statistics are divided by nineties played. Quantities published as rates are used
as published rather than divided a second time. Minutes and appearances are retained as
context and never enter the feature set, because squad usage describes a player's
opportunity rather than the role he performs within it.

Several features are undefined for some players by construction rather than by data loss: a
player who attempted no shots has no shot accuracy, and a player who attempted no take-ons
has no take-on success rate. After the minutes filter these affect at most eight percent of
players in any single feature, and the affected values are median-imputed within position
group and flagged.

\subsection{Adjusting defensive counts for possession}
\label{sec:data:padj}

Defensive actions can only be recorded while the opponent has the ball, so their raw rates
confound what a player does with how often his team is out of possession, which in turn
tracks team strength. The correction and the reasoning behind its exact form are set out in
Section~\ref{sec:methods:padj}. The point for the data description is that the five
opponent-ball counts, tackles, interceptions, blocks, clearances and fouls committed, enter
the feature set in adjusted form.

\subsection{A second, deliberately impoverished sample}
\label{sec:data:reduced}

A separate sample is collected directly from the live site for the seasons ending 2025 and
2026, after the withdrawal. It is not used for the main analysis. Its purpose is a
robustness check: it contains roughly half the features of the primary sample, having lost
expected goals, all passing and possession volume, shot and goal creating actions and the
entire advanced goalkeeping table, and it therefore allows the central result to be
re-derived on a much poorer description of the same game.

That sample carries a methodological warning worth recording independently of this study.
The affected tables are still served with complete column headers and one row per player,
and only the values are gone, so a feature set assembled from the site's published schema
resolves correctly and yields an empty matrix. Table~\ref{tab:availability} reports the
audit. Feature selection against observed values rather than documentation is now a
necessary step when working with this source. The withdrawal was also applied unevenly: at
season level the columns were emptied, while at match level they were removed from the
schema outright, so match reports remain usable for what survived, which
Section~\ref{sec:results:drift} exploits.

\subsection{Known limitations}

Season aggregates discard context. A tackle count does not say where on the pitch the
tackles happened, and an expected goals total does not distinguish six poor chances from
two good ones. The possession correction described above reaches only the opponent-ball
statistics, so attacking output remains related to a team's own share of the ball, and
Section~\ref{sec:results:symmetric} sets out why correcting that would remove signal rather
than bias. Per-90 rates computed over partial seasons remain noisy, which motivates the
shrinkage analysis in Section~\ref{sec:results:shrinkage}. The sample is recent European
club football, so nothing here should be read as a claim about the game in other eras or at
other levels. Finally, the clusters are descriptive and carry no causal content: a cluster
label records how a player's season looked, not why.
