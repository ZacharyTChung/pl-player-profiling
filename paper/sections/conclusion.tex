\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The withdrawal of commercially licensed statistics from the largest free football data
source in January 2026 removed most of the vocabulary that public role analysis had come to
depend on. This paper measured what remains. Merging the surviving statistics with an
independent expected goals source yields \NFeatures{} usable features, and on those
features a role discovery pipeline recovers a single robust division between attacking and
defensive involvement, which cuts across listed positions rather than reproducing them, and
little else that survives scrutiny.

The negative results are the substantive ones. The selection rule returns two clusters in
every scope. Density based clustering, which is free to conclude that no clusters exist,
concludes exactly that within every position group. Bootstrap resampling, cross-season
replication and empirical Bayes shrinkage together show that the defender partition is
substantially a description of sampling noise, and that the forward partition is not
reproducible enough to characterise individuals. The
goalkeeper partition recovers team strength rather than goalkeeping. Reporting these as
findings rather than suppressing them is the point: a study of this kind run without the
stability checks would have produced six confidently named archetypes and a goalkeeper
taxonomy, and most of it would have been wrong.

Two results are strong enough to carry forward. Correlations between statistics reverse
sign across position groups often enough that pooled correlation analysis of football data
should be considered unsafe by default. Club level summaries built from the same features
correlate with final league position at almost identical strength across two independent
seasons, so aggregation to the club recovers stability that individual players do not
have.

The broader lesson concerns dependence. An entire layer of public football analysis rested
on a single source holding a single commercial licence, and when that licence ended the
layer went with it. The work here is reproducible from a public pipeline, which is what
made it possible to measure the loss precisely rather than estimate it, and that
reproducibility is worth more now than it was before January 2026.
