\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The question this paper set out to answer is whether football player roles, as season
aggregate statistics describe them, form types or a continuum. The answer is that they form
neither cleanly, and that the useful description sits between the two: an ordered space with
one dominant axis, density concentrated at two ends, and a populated middle that no
partition divides well.

Two modes are real. Against \StrSimulations{} datasets matched to the observed data on size,
dimension and covariance but built to contain no groups, the two-mode partition is better
separated than every simulation in every scope, and the leading component fails a test of
unimodality outside the forward group. Nothing finer survives the same test. Beyond two modes
the observed separation converges on what a clusterless continuum produces, density based
clustering assigns every player to noise within each position group, and the mixture
criterion prefers ever more components without settling, which is the signature of a
distribution being approximated rather than a set of groups being found.

The two modes are not the listed positions under another name. They agree with position group
at an adjusted Rand index of \ArcARIvsPosition{}, which is far above chance and far below
recovery, and the shape of the disagreement is the finding. Defenders and forwards are
assigned to opposite sides almost deterministically while midfielders divide close to evenly,
so the partition cuts across the listed categories at their busiest point. A supervised model
trained on the same features reaches \SupGBMAcc{} percent accuracy and yet sends all but
\SupPoleShare{} percent of its errors through midfield, which is the same geometry recovered
by machinery that shares nothing with the clustering.

The result is not local. It reproduces in every one of \ArcNSeasons{} seasons and every one of
\ArcNLeagues{} competitions, refitted independently in each, with a worst case agreement of
\RepSeasonPooledMin{} against the pooled partition and a separating axis that correlates with
the pooled axis above \RepAxisCorrMin{} everywhere. Centroids fitted in one league assign
another league's players at a mean adjusted Rand index of \RepTransferMean{}. Where season
against season agreement is lower, it decays with the gap between seasons while the transfer
index over the same pairs stays flat, so the decay measures players changing rather than the
solution failing.

Three secondary results are worth carrying forward. Possession adjustment of defensive counts
is conventionally applied as though a team with twice the ball concedes half the defensive
opportunities, and the elasticities measured here run from \ArcElastTackles{} to
\ArcElastClearances{}, so the conventional correction overcorrects every statistic it touches.
The adjustment is also one sided: attacking output does not scale with possession the way the
symmetric argument would require, and applying the correction in both directions makes the
description worse. Goalkeeping, analysed separately, divides along what a goalkeeper is asked
to do rather than how well he does it, with the situational axis carrying an effect size of
\ArcKeeperEtaFullSituation{} against \ArcKeeperEtaFullTechnique{} for technique.

What follows for practice is a caution about method rather than about football. Published
role taxonomies are typically supported by a selection criterion, a stability check and a
plausible reading of the resulting profiles, and every one of those is satisfied here by
partitions this paper shows to be artefacts. A silhouette maximum, a high bootstrap adjusted
Rand index and an interpretable centroid are all obtainable from data containing no groups
whatsoever, because the algorithm supplies the groups. The calibration against matched
clusterless data is cheap, it needs no assumption the analysis does not already make, and it
is the step that separates the two-mode result reported here from the finer taxonomy that the
same pipeline would otherwise have produced with equal confidence.
