\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result is easy to overstate in either direction. It does not say that football has two
kinds of player, and it does not say that role is a single number. It says that the
distribution of players over \ArcFeatures{} measured statistics has two regions of concentrated
density joined by a populated middle, that the axis running between them is the dominant
direction in the space, and that no division finer than that one is distinguishable from what a
partitioning algorithm produces on data containing no divisions. That is a description a
mixture representation expresses well and a taxonomy does not, which is why the archetypal and
partial membership approaches to athlete description are better matched to this data than the
genre that dominates it \citep{eugster2012performance, seri2024partial}.

The axis itself is territorial rather than technical. Its strongest loadings are touches by
pitch third and progressive receptions, not passing accuracy or duel success, and the same
ordering appears in the supervised attributions, where \ShapArcTopOne{} carries more weight
than any technical statistic for all three position groups. Football's own vocabulary for role
mixes territory with technique freely, describing a player as a ball-playing centre back or a
pressing forward, but the season aggregate record is far better at recording where a player was
than what he did there. Any role structure recovered from it will be mostly a statement about
territory, and reading it as a statement about ability is the most common way to misuse this
kind of analysis. That the discovered division cuts across the listed positions rather than
reproducing them is consistent with what tracking and event data show about modern roles, where
positional assignment is fluid within a match and formations are recovered as distributions
rather than as slots \citep{bialkowski2014large, decroos2019player}.

\subsection{Why finer taxonomies keep being published}

The methodological point generalises past football. A published role taxonomy is typically
supported by three things: a selection criterion that picks the reported number of clusters, a
stability check that shows the partition survives resampling, and centroid profiles that read
sensibly against domain knowledge. Every one of those is satisfied here by partitions this
paper shows to be artefacts.

None of the three tests existence. A selection criterion compares candidate partitions against
each other and is never offered the option of returning none, which is the limitation the
stopping-rule literature has documented since it began \citep{milligan1985examination,
rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. A bootstrap measures whether an algorithm applied twice to similar
data behaves similarly, which a deterministic procedure on a smooth unimodal distribution does
very reliably, exactly as the theory of stability-based validation predicts
\citep{benhur2002stability, vonluxburg2010stability}. Interpretability is the weakest of the
three, because a cut through any continuous distribution produces two centroids that differ
along the direction of the cut, and a domain expert can narrate that difference whether or not
it corresponds to a real boundary. The evidence here makes the concern measured rather than
hypothetical: on data built to contain no groups, the same pipeline returns a silhouette
maximum at two clusters, two nameable centroids, and a bootstrap adjusted Rand index of at
least \StrBootNullMinSingle{} on every clusterless dataset in every scope, against
\StrBootObsMin{} on the real data, a difference as small as \StrBootGapMin{}. A stability check
that passes a continuum at \StrBootNullMinSingle{} is not a test of existence, and a taxonomy
that rests on one rests on evidence a continuum satisfies equally well. Given that 55 percent
of sports science clustering studies do not state how the number of clusters was chosen at all,
the gap between the applied genre and the validity literature is wide
\citep{fernandez2024reporting}.

Calibration against matched clusterless data closes it at low cost. It requires no assumption
the analysis does not already make, since the simulated data are drawn using the observed mean
and covariance; it costs a few dozen refits; and it is the single step that separates the
two-mode result reported here from the six-way taxonomy the same pipeline would have produced
with the same apparent confidence. The gap statistic already does a version of this against a
uniform reference \citep{tibshirani2001estimating}, and what this paper adds is not a new
statistic but harder references and a reporting convention, that the observed value be placed
against all of them so a reader can see which the claim survives. Hartigan's dip test and a
density-based method permitted to answer that nothing is clustered are useful supplements
\citep{hartigan1985dip, adolfsson2019cluster, campello2013density}, and the agreement of all
three is what makes the negative half of the result credible. The position is the one Hennig
argues for, that a partition should be judged by whether it has the properties the analyst
needs rather than by whether it is correct \citep{hennig2015true}; the property asked for here
is that a partition be distinguishable from one imposed on a continuum.

The comparison with the closest prior work is instructive. Akhanli and Hennig's aggregated
validity index, applied to a season of player statistics from eight European leagues, returned
a coarse partition into five ordered groups running along the same territorial axis found here
\citep{akhanli2020comparing, akhanli2023clustering}. Two independent calibrations, one against
random partitions of the observed data and one against clusterless data matched to it, agree
that the recoverable structure in season aggregates is coarse and ordered. The taxonomies of a
dozen named types that the applied genre reports are not what either calibration supports
\citep{elfoukahi2026unveiling, plakias2023identifying, alagappan2012from, kalman2020nba}.

\subsection{What the modes mean in practice}

The two modes are not the listed positions renamed, and where they disagree is the
interesting part. The division leaves the two extreme groups intact and cuts the group between
them close to in half, and the players it reassigns are recognisable: holding midfielders sit
with the defenders and attacking midfielders with the forwards. That the supervised model makes
its errors in the same place is corroboration from machinery sharing nothing with the
clustering. The practical reading is that listed position is a reasonable but coarse summary
at the extremes and close to arbitrary in the middle, which is what anyone who has watched a
modern midfield would expect and which the data now support quantitatively.

The test against real outcomes closes the gap between describing the statistics and describing
the sport, and it closes it in two directions at once. The two-mode composition of a club
carries information about its points that possession does not, out of sample under three
different holdouts, so the axis the partition cuts is one along which football is actually
decided. The six archetypes carry no information about points that the two modes did not
already carry, so the finer taxonomy fails against results exactly as it failed against the
clusterless null. Those are independent tests, one internal and one external, and they agree.
Two cautions belong with that. The composition alone predicts slightly worse than possession
alone, so the increment is the right thing to cite. The within-league correlation also varies,
from \OutRhoLeagueMin{} in \OutRhoLeagueMinName{} to \OutRhoLeagueMax{} in
\OutRhoLeagueMaxName{}, a reminder that the same composition buys different amounts in
different competitions.

For recruitment the honest use of this work is narrow and real. The involvement axis is stable
enough to support questions about squad composition, such as whether a club's minutes are
concentrated at one end of the distribution. What the work does not support is a scouting
taxonomy, since a shortlist built by matching a target's archetype label matches a label the
data do not establish, and a similarity search over the continuous profile is the better
instrument for the same question because it does not force a boundary that is not there. For
anyone doing quantitative football analysis the two directly transferable items are the
possession elasticities, which apply to any defensive count comparison and correct a convention
that has stood since it was proposed without being measured \citep{knutson2014possession}, and
the null calibration, which costs little and should be run before any archetype claim is made.

The goalkeeper analysis carries its own caution. The division that emerges runs along what a
goalkeeper is asked to do rather than how well he does it, and a partition of goalkeepers built
from the full public feature block is mostly a partition of the teams in front of them, with a
situational effect size of \ArcKeeperEtaFullSituation{} against \ArcKeeperEtaFullTechnique{}
for technique. Anyone building goalkeeper archetypes from public data will produce labels that
describe a role assignment made by a coach rather than a property of the goalkeeper, and it is
easy to present the former as the latter. Conditioning on shot difficulty through post-shot
expected goals rather than on shot volume is the minimum requirement for saying anything about
shot stopping at all \citep{vatvani2022upgrading}.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

Season aggregates discard context. A tackle count does not say where the tackles happened, an
expected goals total does not distinguish six poor chances from two good ones, and a touch map
summarised into three thirds is a coarse instrument for a question that turns out to be
territorial. Everything here is a claim about what season aggregate data can support, not about
what football contains, and the two should not be conflated: it remains entirely possible that
finer roles exist and that this description cannot see them. Event and tracking data would be
the way to find out \citep{pappalardo2019playerank, aalbers2019distinguishing}.

The confound that possession adjustment does not remove is team strength. Correcting the
defensive counts removes the exposure effect from those five features, but attacking statistics
still scale with a team's own share of the ball, so the leading component remains related to
team quality. The unit of analysis is the player-season, and player-seasons are nested, with the
same player appearing in up to \ArcNSeasons{} rows and clubs nested within leagues. The pooled
fits and the bootstrap treat rows as exchangeable, which they are not, so pooled intervals are
narrower than a fully nested analysis would give. The defence against this is design rather
than adjustment: every central claim is also made on independent refits by season and by
league, and the test against results holds out whole seasons, whole leagues and whole clubs.
The archive is a mirror rather than the source, and although its coverage was audited it cannot
be re-derived from the live site, and one season was excluded outright because a whole feature
block was absent from it. Season aggregation also assumes a player's role is constant across a
season, which the drift analysis of Section~\ref{sec:si:reduced} shows holds for most players
and fails for a minority.

Three directions follow. Event and tracking data would restore the spatial detail that season
aggregates discard and would test the two-mode claim against a description rich enough to
reveal finer structure if it exists. The most interesting version of that test is the one this
paper cannot run, whether the modes are a property of football or a property of the
measurement, which requires a dataset in which a finer taxonomy is known to be present.
Extending beyond the big five, to competitions with different possession profiles, would test
whether the elasticities and the two-mode result hold where the league is less concentrated.

The question this paper set out to answer is whether football player roles, as season aggregate
statistics describe them, form types or a continuum. They form neither cleanly. The useful
description sits between the two: an ordered space with one dominant axis, density concentrated
at two ends, and a populated middle that no partition divides well. Two modes are real, they
reproduce in every season and every competition, they predict what happened on the pitch, and
nothing finer than them survives a test that structureless data would fail. What follows for
practice is a caution about method rather than about football. A silhouette maximum, a high
bootstrap adjusted Rand index and an interpretable centroid are all obtainable from data
containing no groups whatsoever, because the algorithm supplies the groups.
