\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result is easy to overstate in either direction. It does not say football has two kinds of
player, and it does not say role is a single number. It says the distribution of players over
\ArcFeatures{} measured statistics has two regions of concentrated density joined by a populated
middle, that the axis running between them is the dominant direction in the space, and that no
division finer than that one is distinguishable from what a partitioning algorithm produces on
data containing no divisions. That is a description a mixture representation expresses well and a
taxonomy does not, which is why the archetypal and partial membership approaches to athlete
description fit this data better than the genre that dominates it \citep{eugster2012performance,
seri2024partial}.

The axis is territorial rather than technical. Its strongest loadings are touches by pitch third
and progressive receptions, not passing accuracy or duel success, and the same ordering appears in
the supervised attributions. Football's own vocabulary mixes territory with technique freely,
describing a player as a ball-playing centre back or a pressing forward, but the season aggregate
record is far better at recording where a player was than what he did there. Any role structure
recovered from it will be mostly a statement about territory, and reading it as a statement about
ability is the most common way to misuse this kind of analysis. That the division cuts across the
listed positions rather than reproducing them is consistent with what tracking and event data show
about modern roles, where positional assignment is fluid within a match and formations are
recovered as distributions rather than as slots \citep{bialkowski2014large, decroos2019player}.

\subsection{Why finer taxonomies keep being published}

The methodological point generalises past football. A published role taxonomy is typically
supported by three things: a selection criterion that picks the reported number of clusters, a
stability check showing the partition survives resampling, and centroid profiles that read
sensibly against domain knowledge. Every one of those is satisfied here by partitions this paper
shows to be artefacts, because none of the three tests existence. A selection criterion compares
candidate partitions against each other and is never offered the option of returning none, which
is the limitation the stopping-rule literature has documented since it began
\citep{milligan1985examination, rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. A bootstrap measures whether an
algorithm applied twice to similar data behaves similarly, which a deterministic procedure on a
smooth unimodal distribution does very reliably, exactly as the theory of stability-based
validation predicts \citep{benhur2002stability, vonluxburg2010stability}. Interpretability is the
weakest of the three, because a cut through any continuous distribution produces two centroids
that differ along the direction of the cut, and a domain expert can narrate that difference
whether or not it corresponds to a real boundary. The evidence here makes the concern measured
rather than hypothetical: on data built to contain no groups the same pipeline returns a
silhouette maximum at two clusters, two nameable centroids, and a bootstrap adjusted Rand index of
at least \StrBootNullMinSingle{} in every scope, a difference from the real data as small as
\StrBootGapMin{}. Given that 55 percent of sports science clustering studies do not state how the
number of clusters was chosen at all, the gap between the applied genre and the validity
literature is wide \citep{fernandez2024reporting}.

Calibration against matched clusterless data closes it at low cost. It requires no assumption the
analysis does not already make, it costs a few dozen refits, and it is the single step separating
the two-mode result reported here from the six-way taxonomy the same pipeline would have produced
with the same apparent confidence. The gap statistic already does a version of this against a
uniform reference \citep{tibshirani2001estimating}, and what this paper adds is not a new
statistic but harder references and a reporting convention, that the observed value be placed
against all of them so a reader can see which the claim survives. Hartigan's dip test and a
density-based method permitted to answer that nothing is clustered are useful supplements
\citep{hartigan1985dip, adolfsson2019cluster, campello2013density}, and the agreement of all three
is what makes the negative half of the result credible. The position is the one Hennig argues for,
that a partition should be judged by whether it has the properties the analyst needs rather than
by whether it is correct \citep{hennig2015true}; the property asked for here is that a partition
be distinguishable from one imposed on a continuum. The comparison with the closest prior work
supports it. Akhanli and Hennig's aggregated validity index, calibrated against random partitions
of the observed data rather than against clusterless data, returned a coarse partition into five
ordered groups running along the same territorial axis found here \citep{akhanli2020comparing,
akhanli2023clustering}. Two independent calibrations agree that the recoverable structure in
season aggregates is coarse and ordered, and that the taxonomies of a dozen named types the
applied genre reports are not what either supports \citep{elfoukahi2026unveiling,
plakias2023identifying, alagappan2012from, kalman2020nba}.

\subsection{What follows in practice}

The division leaves the two extreme position groups intact and cuts the group between them close
to in half, and the players it reassigns are recognisable: holding midfielders sit with the
defenders and attacking midfielders with the forwards. That the supervised model errs in the same
place is corroboration from machinery sharing nothing with the clustering. Listed position is
therefore a reasonable but coarse summary at the extremes and close to arbitrary in the middle,
which is what anyone who has watched a modern midfield would expect and which the data now support
quantitatively.

The test against real outcomes closes the gap between describing the statistics and describing the
sport, and it closes it in two directions at once. The two-mode composition of a club carries
information about its points that possession does not, out of sample under three different
holdouts, so the axis the partition cuts is one along which football is actually decided. The six
archetypes carry no information about points the two modes did not already carry, so the finer
taxonomy fails against results exactly as it failed against the clusterless null. Those are
independent tests, one internal and one external, and they agree. Two cautions belong with that:
the composition alone predicts slightly worse than possession alone, so the increment is the right
thing to cite, and the within-league correlation varies from \OutRhoLeagueMin{} in
\OutRhoLeagueMinName{} to \OutRhoLeagueMax{} in \OutRhoLeagueMaxName{}.

For recruitment the honest use of this work is narrow and real. The involvement axis is stable
enough to support questions about squad composition, such as whether a club's minutes are
concentrated at one end of the distribution. What it does not support is a scouting taxonomy,
since a shortlist built by matching a target's archetype label matches a label the data do not
establish, and a similarity search over the continuous profile is the better instrument because it
does not force a boundary that is not there. The two directly transferable items are the
possession elasticities, which apply to any defensive count comparison and correct a convention
that has stood since it was proposed without being measured \citep{knutson2014possession}, and the
null calibration, which costs little and should be run before any archetype claim is made. The
goalkeeper analysis carries its own caution: a partition built from the full public feature block
is mostly a partition of the teams in front of the goalkeepers, so anyone building goalkeeper
archetypes from public data will produce labels describing a role assignment made by a coach
rather than a property of the goalkeeper, and conditioning on shot difficulty through post-shot
expected goals is the minimum requirement for saying anything about shot stopping at all
\citep{vatvani2022upgrading}.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

Season aggregates discard context. A tackle count does not say where the tackles happened, an
expected goals total does not distinguish six poor chances from two good ones, and a touch map
summarised into three thirds is a coarse instrument for a question that turns out to be
territorial. Everything here is a claim about what season aggregate data can support, not about
what football contains, and the two should not be conflated: finer roles may well exist where this
description cannot see them, and event and tracking data would be the way to find out
\citep{pappalardo2019playerank, aalbers2019distinguishing}. The confound possession adjustment
does not remove is team strength, since attacking statistics still scale with a team's own share
of the ball. The unit of analysis is the player-season and player-seasons are nested, with the
same player appearing in up to \ArcNSeasonsWord{} rows and clubs nested within leagues, so pooled
intervals are narrower than a fully nested analysis would give; the defence against this is design
rather than adjustment, since every central claim is also made on independent refits by season and
by league and the test against results holds out whole seasons, leagues and clubs. The archive is
a mirror rather than the source, and one season was excluded outright because a whole feature
block was absent from it. Season aggregation also assumes a player's role is constant across a
season, which holds for most players and fails for a minority.

The question this paper set out to answer is whether football player roles, as season aggregate
statistics describe them, form types or a continuum. They form neither cleanly. The useful
description sits between the two: an ordered space with one dominant axis, density concentrated at
two ends, and a populated middle that no partition divides well. Two modes are real, they
reproduce in every season and every competition, they predict what happened on the pitch, and
nothing finer survives a test that structureless data would fail. What follows for practice is a
caution about method rather than about football, because a silhouette maximum, a high bootstrap
adjusted Rand index and an interpretable centroid are all obtainable from data containing no
groups whatsoever: the algorithm supplies the groups.
